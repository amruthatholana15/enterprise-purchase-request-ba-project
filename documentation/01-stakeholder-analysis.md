# Stakeholder Analysis

## Purpose

The purpose of stakeholder analysis was to identify the key participants involved in the purchase request approval process and understand their responsibilities, pain points, and business needs.

## Key Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Role in Process | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Requesters / Employees | Create and submit purchase requests | Limited visibility into approval status and uncertainty around required information/documents |
| Department Managers | Review and approve business need | Generally review requests quickly but may manually follow up with downstream approval teams |
| Finance Approvers | Validate budget, cost center, amount and financial information | Requests may be returned due to incorrect cost centers, missing budget information, missing quotations or amount mismatches |
| Procurement Specialists | Validate purchasing, vendor and supporting-document requirements | Requests may be returned due to incomplete scope, vendor information, purchase description, category or quotation |
| Compliance | Perform conditional compliance review | Involved only when applicable based on defined business conditions |
| Senior Management | Provide additional approval when required | Approval is conditional based on applicable business rules |
| ProcureFlow Application Owner | Manages workflow/application functionality | Confirmed conditional routing, workflow timestamps, fragmented guidance and limited validation/reminder functionality |

## Stakeholder Elicitation Approach

Stakeholder-style interviews were used to understand:

- Current purchase request submission process
- Approval responsibilities
- Typical processing timelines
- Common reasons for returns
- Approval routing
- Workarounds and manual follow-ups
- System limitations
- Documentation and visibility challenges

## Key Themes Identified

1. Submission requirements are not always clear to requesters.
2. Finance and Procurement frequently identify incomplete or incorrect information after submission.
3. Requesters have limited visibility into where requests are pending.
4. Managers may manually follow up with approval teams.
5. Approval routing varies depending on purchase characteristics.
6. Existing process guidance is fragmented across multiple sources.
7. ProcureFlow does not consistently enforce all applicable information and document requirements before submission.
